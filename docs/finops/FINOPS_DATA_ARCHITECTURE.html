Helena FinOps — Data Model Architecture

The data structure behind SEK's IT cost-management platform — controlled dimensions, planned-vs-actual fact tables, transactional FX, and the reports they power. Scope: the finops_* domain only.

Cost Management · Single-tenant · Multi-currency

Evidence the delta between projected and real IT spend,
and prove savings — across currencies and dimensions.

FinOps separates what we plan to spend (obligations) from what we actually spend (transactions), normalizes every amount to a single base currency at the moment of record, and classifies everything against eight controlled catalogs aligned to the SEK Cost Classification Standard v1.0 (servicio · país · cliente · tipo de costo). That clean separation is what makes the projection, GAP, variance, and savings-realization reports trustworthy.

Overview — the shape of the model

The problem

IT cost data arrives in many currencies, from many sources (SAP, cards, cloud bills, manual entry), tagged inconsistently. Leadership needs to answer: are we on budget, where is the variance, and did our savings initiatives actually land? Raw invoices can't answer that.

The solution

Three design choices make the reports reliable:

  • Planned vs Actual separation. CostItem holds an obligation (no provenance); RealSpendRecord holds a transaction (full provenance). They never blend.
  • Transactional FX. Every monetary row stores its original amount and a frozen base-currency value (baseAmount + fxRate + fxRateDate), so historical reports never drift when rates change.
  • Controlled dimensions. Eight catalogs (country, service, client, category…) are the only allowed tags — every report can group consistently.
  • SEK classification & governance. The client dimension (clientClassification + a Service-Operation-Hub-backed ClientCatalog), approved DistributionFormula references for factorizable costs, and investment/CAPEX approval rules make the four-dimension standard enforceable.

High-level entity relationships

The financial core: two fact tables (CostItem, RealSpendRecord) both reference the eight catalogs as dimensions. CostAllocation spreads a transversal cost item across a dimension (service, country or client); DistributionFormula is the approved prorrateo rule those allocations reference; SavingsInitiative links to cost items it intends to reduce; ExchangeRate feeds the base-currency freeze on every monetary row.

1 · Controlled classification catalogs

Eight small lookup tables provide the only allowed tags for costs and spend. Each shares the same shape: a stable id, a unique short code, a human name, and a soft-delete flag (isActive). They are never deleted — deactivation preserves history. "Country", "Business Unit" and "Client" here are cost dimensions, not the support system's organizations. For the Client catalog the code is the official Service Operation Hub (CMDB) client/contract ID — no local identifiers (SEK standard §9).

Used in reports

Every grouped report (groupBy=country|service|client|category|…) resolves its buckets through these catalogs. The breakdown dashboard groups planned cost by tipo-costo/country/category/client; accumulated spend and GAP group actual spend by any dimension. The Service, Country and Client catalogs participate in transversal-cost spreading — a CostAllocation row's targetId is resolved (per its dimension) against the matching catalog rather than by a hard foreign key.

2 · CostItem finops_cost_items

The planned / expected obligation — what we intend to spend. It carries no provenance (it is not a transaction). Its recurrence and vigencia (validity window) let the projection engine expand it into dated payment occurrences; its classification flags drive the COGS/SG&A/CAPEX and recurring/licenses/investment splits.

3 · CostAllocation finops_cost_allocations

Prorrateo: when a planned cost is transversal / factorizable, this table spreads it across one or more targets of a chosen dimension by percentage. The SEK standard's factorizable codes map to the three allowed dimensions: SVC-*/*-TRV → SERVICE, REG-FAC → COUNTRY, CLI-FAC → CLIENT. Percentages for a given cost item must sum to 100 within each dimension (or be empty). Uniqueness on (costItemId, dimension, targetId) prevents double-weighting.

FieldTypeDescription
idString (uuid)Primary key.
costItemIdString (FK)The transversal cost item being spread (cascade delete).
dimensionenum AllocationDimension = SERVICESERVICE | COUNTRY | CLIENT — which dimension this share prorrates.
targetIdStringId of the service/country/client catalog entry (polymorphic; validated in the service layer against the active catalog, no hard FK).
percentageDecimal(7,4)Share of the cost item allocated to this target (0–100).
formulaIdString? (FK)Approved DistributionFormula backing this split (SEK formula_ref).
createdAt / updatedAtDateTimeAudit timestamps.

Used in reports

When Projection or GAP is grouped by service, by country or by client, items with allocations for that dimension are split across their targets by percentage weight; items without allocations are attributed whole to their own dimension id. This is what lets a shared platform cost show up proportionally under each service, country or client it supports.

4 · DistributionFormula finops_distribution_formulas

The approved prorrateo rule behind every factorizable (*-FAC/*-TRV/REG-FAC/CLI-FAC) cost — the SEK standard's formula_ref (§8). A formula must be APPROVED by Finance before it is applied; CostItem.formulaId and CostAllocation.formulaId both reference it.

FieldTypeDescription
idString (uuid)Primary key.
name / descriptionString / String?Label and how the distribution is computed.
criterionenum DistributionCriterionCONSUMO (EPS/GB) | TICKETS | HEADCOUNT | AD_HOC (§8).
statusenum DistributionFormulaStatus = DRAFTDRAFT | APPROVED | ARCHIVED.
approvedByUserId / approvedAtString? / DateTime?Finance approver + stamp, set by the approve action.
createdAt / updatedAtDateTimeAudit timestamps.

Used in reports

Not read by the aggregation reports directly — it is a governance/reference entity. It is managed through /api/v3/finops/distribution-formulas (Finance-admin gated, with an approve action) and referenced by cost items and allocations to evidence that every factorizable split uses an approved, documented criterion.

5 · RealSpendRecord finops_real_spend_records

The actual transaction — money that was really spent. It carries full provenance, a frozen base amount, deduplication and reconciliation state, and the same dimension tags as CostItem so the two can be compared head-to-head. Provenance is deliberately split into business origin (how it was paid) and ingestion channel (how the data entered).

  • Accumulated: sum of baseAmount over a window, optionally grouped by dimension.
  • GAP (real side): actual spend per dimension vs the projected total.
  • New-spend & Variance: compares current vs prior windows to surface new cost keys and threshold-crossing deltas.
  • Savings realization: actual base spend over linked items in the target period.
  • Overview executed-YTD: year-to-date base spend KPI.

6 · ExchangeRate finops_exchange_rates

A date-versioned FX table. It is the source for the transactional-FX freeze: when any monetary record is written, the system looks up the rate as-of the relevant date and stores the converted base amount on that record — so the rate is captured once and historical totals never move when rates change later.

FieldTypeDescription
idString (uuid)Primary key.
baseCurrencyVarChar(3)Target/base currency of the conversion.
quoteCurrencyVarChar(3)Source currency being converted.
rateDecimal(18,8)Conversion rate.
rateDateDateTimeEffective date — lookups pick the rate on/before a record's date.
sourceString?Where the rate came from.
Unique on (baseCurrency, quoteCurrency, rateDate); indexed on (quoteCurrency, rateDate).

Used in reports

Indirectly powers every report: because conversion is frozen at write time, projection, GAP, accumulated and savings all simply sum baseAmount — no live conversion, no drift. Savings realization additionally re-freezes baseline/expected amounts as-of the initiative's target-period start for comparability.

7 · SavingsInitiative & SavingsInitiativeItem

Tracks cost-reduction initiatives and proves they landed. The core formula is realized = baseline − actual, measured over the actual spend of the linked cost items during the target period. The join table links an initiative to the specific obligations it intends to reduce.

Used in reports

Savings realization sums actual base spend over the linked items in the target period, computes realized = baseline − actual and a compliance percent vs expected, and caches the result on the initiative. The Overview dashboard rolls up realizedSavingsAmount across initiatives into the savings-goal progress.

Operational plumbing (not part of the reporting model). Four tables support the import pipeline and audit but are not read by any report: SpendImportBatch (file upload + row counts), ExecutionTracker (async upload→Airflow→agent-fix→insert orchestration state), SpendBatchAttempt (idempotency ledger for batch inserts), and FinopsAuditEvent (immutable change trail). They populate and protect RealSpendRecord; they don't appear in projection, GAP, variance or savings outputs. Intentionally omitted from the field-level detail above.

Design principles that make the reports trustworthy

Planned vs Actual

CostItem (obligation, no provenance) and RealSpendRecord (transaction, full provenance) never blend — the GAP report depends on this split.

Transactional FX

Base amount is frozen on write with the rate & date captured. Historical totals never drift when FX changes.

Split provenance

paymentOrigin (how it was paid) is distinct from ingestionChannel (how data arrived) — two independent lenses.

Multi-dimension allocation

Transversal costs spread across services, countries or clients by weighted CostAllocation (polymorphic dimension+targetId); un-allocated costs stay whole.

Fingerprint idempotency

A unique sourceFingerprint blocks double-counting; batch attempts are idempotent on replay.

Derived accounting & tipo-costo

Accounting type derives CAPEX → COGS → SG&A; cost type investment → licenses → recurring; SEK tipo-costo CAPEX → OPEX-INV → OPEX-GST — all from existing fields.

SEK four-dimension standard

Servicio · país · cliente · tipo de costo. Client is a first-class catalog keyed on the Service Operation Hub ID; factorizable costs carry an approved DistributionFormula.

Investment governance

OPEX-INV/CAPEX needs a C-Level approval reference or it is coerced to OPEX-GST; CAPEX/OPEX-INV forces cliente = N/A unless a Finance-approved exception.
